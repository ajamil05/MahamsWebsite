Synopsis

People Are Stupid: The Lies We Believe to Make It Make Sense

“Most people don’t lack intelligence – they lack the courage to see clearly.”

People Are Stupid is not an attack on intelligence. It’s a closer look at something far more common: the quiet, daily art of self-deception.

At its core, the book makes a simple argument: human beings aren’t really driven by truth. We’re driven by the need to protect our identity. When life gets uncomfortable, when we lose something, when reality contradicts the story we’ve been telling ourselves, the mind doesn’t reach for accuracy – it reaches for coherence. It builds explanations that soften the blow, justify the choice, and keep the ego intact.

This isn’t a perspective Maham Khalid arrived at through theory. She arrived at it the hard way – through the loss of both her parents in quick succession: her mother to cancer, her father to heart disease. Grief like that doesn’t leave room for the usual stories. It strips them away whether you’re ready or not. And in that stripped-down space, the patterns she’d spent years observing in other people suddenly became impossible to ignore in herself.

What was left was clarity – the kind most people spend a lifetime working around. This book came out of that clarity.

Written as a quiet, deliberate letter to her children, People Are Stupid is an exploration of the stories we tell ourselves, the choices we defend, and the truths we avoid.

It is ultimately a book about self-awareness, courage and the uncomfortable freedom that comes from seeing ourselves clearly.
